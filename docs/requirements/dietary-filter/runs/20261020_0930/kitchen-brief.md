# Brief — filter the weekly menu by diet
<!-- COPIED IN, UNCHANGED, under the header above. Every citation in dietary-filter/requirements.md resolves against this copy. -->

## Why we are asking

Customer care answers about forty contacts a week from customers who cannot tell which of this
week's recipes suit them. Most of them are vegetarian or vegan. They scroll the whole menu, open
each recipe, and read the ingredients. Some give up and do not order.

## What we need

A customer must be able to show only the recipes of this week's menu that suit one diet. The
diets we start with are vegetarian and vegan, which the kitchen already tags on every recipe.
Showing the full menu again must be one step.

If a customer asks for a diet we do not offer, we must say so. We must never show a menu that
looks filtered when it is not.

A customer who filters the menu and then reloads the page, or sends the link to their partner,
must see the same filtered menu.

The menu must stay in the kitchen's order when it is filtered. The kitchen puts the recipe of the
week first on purpose.

## Gluten-free

Customer care also asks for a gluten-free filter. The kitchen does not tag any recipe gluten-free
today, because a recipe is gluten-free only when the food safety lead certifies the whole supply
chain for it. We want the filter, but only for certified recipes.

## What we do not need

Combining two diets in one filter, such as vegan and gluten-free together. Nobody has asked for it.

Allergen information beyond the diet tags. That is a separate piece of work with Legal.

## Constraints

The filter must not change the prices or the content of a recipe. It only hides recipes.

## Not yet agreed

Who certifies a recipe as gluten-free, and how the certification reaches the catalogue.
